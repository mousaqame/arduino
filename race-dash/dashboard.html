Only two of these four ever reach the board

TRIM and SPAN are the firmware's own commands: the servo moves as you change them, and both live in the board's RAM, so a reset, a reflash or a reconnect throws them away. ZERO and FULL move this page's model of the needle and the dial drawn under it. Nothing here is permanent until the numbers are in the sketch and the board has been flashed again — which is what the block at the bottom of this tab is for.

      Every pin on the Uno, and why the empty ones are empty. The board runs one 16×2 character
      LCD wired straight to the digital pins, one 128×32 OLED on the fixed I2C pair, and two
      servos driven in raw microseconds.
    


    

      The trap that catches everyone
      

        The LCD has pins printed D4 D5 D6 D7 on it. Those do not go to the
        Arduino pins of the same number — they go to 5, 4, 3 and 2, in that order, which
        is backwards from what you would guess. Go by the LCD's own pin numbers: count
        from pin 1 at the end marked VSS/GND on the silkscreen.

      Servo power does not come from the board
      

        Never run a servo from the Uno's 5V pin. An MG90S pulls around 700 mA stalled and
        a few hundred just snapping the needle over; a Uno on USB has about 500 mA for the whole
        board. Give the servos their own 5V 2A supply with a 1000 µF capacitor across it, and tie
        that supply's ground to the board's ground. The shared ground wire is not optional.
      

    


    

      Why the two needles take turns
      

        The firmware ticks every 20 ms but moves only one gauge per tick, so each needle
        still refreshes at 25 Hz while the two servos never draw their step current in the same
        instant. That halves the worst-case sag on the 5V rail, which is what causes brown-out
        resets. The self-test sweeps them one after the other for the same reason.
      

    


    

      Pins 9 and 10 can never be PWM again
      

        On an AVR Uno the Servo library takes over Timer1, which kills analogWrite()
        on pins 9 and 10. That costs nothing here because those are the servo pins, but
        with the LCD holding 3, 5 and 11, pin 6 is the only PWM output left.
