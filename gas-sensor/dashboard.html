      This sniffs the air for gases like the ones in a lighter, a camping stove, or a gas
      cooker, and sets off a light and a buzzer when it smells too much.
    


    

      Fun to build — not a safety device
      

        This sensor isn't calibrated and isn't certified, so it can't tell you a real gas
        concentration and it can miss things. It's a great thing to build and learn from.
        Do not rely on it to keep anyone safe — a proper certified gas alarm is a separate
        purchase and worth it.

            The confusing one
            

              The gas sensor board has a pin labelled A0 printed on it. That does
              not go to the Arduino's A0 — it goes to A5. The label is the
              sensor's own name for the pin, not a destination.

            The sensor gets warm. That's normal — it has a small heater inside, which is
            how it works. It also means it needs about half a minute after power-up before its
            readings mean anything.
          

          

            Give it a minute before trusting it. The sensor warms up for about half a
            minute, then spends ten seconds measuring the clean air in your room. Everything
            after that is compared against what it learned, so make sure the air really is
            fresh while it does that.

Wait, then test it

          

            The sensor warms up for about half a minute, then measures the clean air in your
            room and remembers it. After that, everything is compared against that reading.
          

          
Send the code first.

          

            To test it safely: hold an unlit lighter near the sensor and press the gas
            lever for a second without lighting it. The reading should jump. Never test with
            a flame, and open a window afterwards.
